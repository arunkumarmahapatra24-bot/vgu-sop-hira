5 × 5 Risk Matrix (RR = C × L)

Low 1–4 · no mitigation requiredMedium 5–10 · controls verified, ALARPSignificant 11–16 · mitigate with priorityHigh 17–25 · mitigate with highest priority

If the hazard is Legal, it is considered High risk irrespective of the score. Only RR < 4 results in an acceptable situation without further action.

Consequence of severity (C)

5Fatality, public hospitalisation, or severe health effects
4Permanent disability, hospitalisation, or major health effects
3One or more lost-time workday cases or significant health effects
2Medical treatment with restricted duty or medium health effects
1Minor health effects, first-aid case, or less

Likelihood of occurrence (L)

5Frequent — once in a week
4Probable — week to year
3Rare — year to 5 years
2Remote — once in 6–20 years
1Improbable — virtually impossible / once in a lifetime (> 20 years)

Consequence scale used in the hazard-type sheet (1–6)

1 Near miss, no injury · 2 First-aid injury, absence of a few hours · 3 Minor injury / short-term illness, absence up to 48 h (sutures) · 4 Major injury / long-term illness, partial or permanent disability (fractures) · 5 Permanent disability or chronic illness (amputation) · 6 Loss of life (fatality)

HIRA Method — 7 Steps

  1. Define the scope — area, equipment or activity from the SOP master list (routine, non-routine, maintenance, emergency).
  2. Identify hazards — mechanical, vehicle interaction, pressure, height, fire / spontaneous combustion, dust, electrical.
  3. Identify cause and consequence — write as Hazard → Cause → Event → Consequence.
  4. Assess the risk — Likelihood (1–5) × Severity (1–5) = initial risk.
  5. Evaluate existing controls — the controls already written into the SOP (interlocks, limit switches, PTW, TBT, supervision, PPE).
  6. Recommend additional controls — hierarchy of controls; PPE last.
  7. Record, implement, review — owner, target date, residual score; review after incidents, MOC, new equipment, and at least annually. SOP revision when ≥ 25 % of the procedure changes or every 3 years.

Hierarchy of Controls

  • 🚫EliminationRemove the hazard — e.g. tipper trucks so the excavator never enters a truck body.
  • 🔄SubstitutionAutomatic samplers instead of manual sampling at height; digital instruments instead of mercury.
  • ⚙️EngineeringLimit switches, photo cells, pull-cords, interlocks (bag filter ↔ tippler), guards, goal posts under OHL, bunds and ramps.
  • 📋AdministrativePTW, tool-box talk, HIRA, key-box control, horn code, speed limit, traffic rules, six-monthly training, service-provider evaluation.
  • 🦺PPEHelmet, goggles, dust mask, HV jacket, harness, ceramic gloves and face hood for fly ash.
